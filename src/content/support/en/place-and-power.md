Stand your Tali where you will actually look at it — on the counter beside the
cellar, on the shelf above the humidor, on the desk in the study. It is the
part of the system you read, so it does not belong inside the space it is
watching. The Puk sensors go inside; the screen stays out.

Plug in the USB-C cable and give it a moment. The e-paper display wakes on its
own and shows its first frame without any setup — that first frame is how you
know the unit is alive before the app is anywhere in the picture.

## About the screen

E-paper holds its image using no power at all, and only draws current when it
changes. That is why the display looks static: it is not refreshing sixty times
a second like a phone, it is redrawing when there is something new to say. A
screen that has not changed in a while is normal. A screen that has not changed
in hours, with a stale timestamp, is worth a look.

Keep the cable in. Tali is a mains-powered instrument, not a battery one — the
battery inside covers moves and brief unplugging, not permanent cordless use.
