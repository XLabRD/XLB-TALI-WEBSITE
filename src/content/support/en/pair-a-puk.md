Each Puk ships with a plastic tab through the battery compartment, holding the
cell off its contacts so it arrives with a full charge. Pull the tab out. That
is the whole power-on procedure — there is no switch and no button to hold.

The Puk starts advertising immediately and shows up in the app within a few
seconds. Accept it, and it pairs to your Tali over Bluetooth Low Energy.

## What is talking to what

A Puk never touches your Wi-Fi. It talks only to the Tali screen, over
Bluetooth, and the screen is the one thing on your network. That is why the Puk
runs for around eighteen months on a coin cell: it is not carrying a Wi-Fi
radio.

One Tali holds up to **ten Puks**. Pair them one at a time, and name each one as
you go — a pile of identically-named sensors is much harder to sort out later
than it is to label now.

## If it does not appear

Bring the Puk within arm's length of the Tali while pairing, then move it to
where it will live. A first pairing at long range through a closed metal door is
the most common reason a new Puk stays invisible.

Check the tab, too. On one of the earliest versions a manufacturing defect can
stop it coming out cleanly, which leaves the battery out of contact —
[Tali isn't detecting a Puk](/support/puk-wont-pair/) covers what to do.
