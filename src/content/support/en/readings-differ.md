Put two thermometers side by side and they will usually differ a little. That is
normal, and it is worth understanding before deciding either one is wrong.

## Give them the same air

A sensor that has just been moved is still reading the place it came from. Both
instruments need time in the same spot before their numbers mean anything next
to each other.

## A cellar is not one temperature

The top of a rack and the floor can differ by a degree or more, and the air near
the cooling unit is not the air around your bottles. Two sensors in the same
cellar but not the same *place* are answering different questions, and both
answers can be right.

## Compare over hours

Look at them across an afternoon, not at a glance. What protects a collection is
stability and trend — a slow drift, a daily swing, a door left open — and those
show up in a line, not in a single reading. A small fixed offset between two
instruments matters far less than either one moving.

## When to write in

If the difference is large, grows over time, or the readings jump around while
another instrument sits still, tell us what you are seeing and we will look at
it with you.
