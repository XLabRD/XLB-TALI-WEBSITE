Tali sends two different kinds of message, and they answer different questions.
**Threshold alerts** say something is wrong inside a zone. **System
notifications** say something is wrong with the system that watches the zones.

## Threshold alerts

A threshold alert fires when a zone leaves the range you set for it — the cellar
climbs past its ceiling, the humidor dries below its floor.

Local alerts appear on the Tali screen itself and are included with every device
for life. Alerts that reach you when you are not in the room are part of Cloud
Pro, included for two years with the Founders Edition.

### They arm on the first good reading

New thresholds do not fire straight away. When you set a zone's limits for the
first time, its alerts stay **disarmed** until the Puk reads a value *inside*
the range you defined. That first in-range reading arms them — and from then on,
if the zone leaves the range, an alert and a notification are reported to the
app.

So a zone that has been out of range since the moment you set its limits has
never armed, and will stay quiet. Set the range around where the zone actually
is now, let the reading settle inside it, and then tighten.

## System notifications

These do not come from a threshold. They tell you the system itself has stopped
doing its job, which is worth knowing sooner than any reading — a monitor that
has gone quiet is not protecting anything.

The app will notify you when:

- **The Tali loses mains power.** Unplugged, or the power went out.
- **The cloud has not heard from your Tali for more than 30 minutes.** Usually
  the network rather than the device.
- **A Puk stops reporting** its data to the Tali. Out of range, or a flat cell —
  see [a Puk has stopped reporting](/support/puk-stopped-reporting/).

## Silencing an alert

Shake the Tali. An alert showing on the screen is dismissed by picking the unit
up and giving it a shake — the same accelerometer that watches for vibration in
your zones is what registers it, so there is no button to hunt for and nothing
to open in the app.

Silencing an alert deals with the notice, not the cause. The zone is still
outside its range until the reading comes back.
