With the app open and your phone next to the Tali, follow the network step. Your
phone reaches the Tali over Bluetooth, you choose your network and type its
password, and the phone passes both to the device. From then on the Tali talks
to your Wi-Fi directly and no longer needs your phone nearby.

## What the Wi-Fi is actually for

Not for measuring. The sensors, the screen and the local alerts all work on
their own, with or without a network. Wi-Fi is what carries readings to the
cloud so you can see them from anywhere, and what brings firmware updates down
to the device.

If the connection drops, Tali keeps measuring and displaying locally and syncs
the whole gap when the network returns. You do not lose history to an outage.

## It has to be 2.4 GHz

Tali only supports **2.4 GHz** networks — it cannot see a 5 GHz one at all. Most
routers publish both, and there is nothing to do. If yours publishes 5 GHz only,
turn the 2.4 GHz network on in its settings before you start; nearly every
router allows it. There is more on this in
[I can't connect my Tali to Wi-Fi](/support/wifi-wont-connect/).

One other network to avoid: a guest network that opens a browser page asking you
to accept terms cannot be joined by a device with no browser.
