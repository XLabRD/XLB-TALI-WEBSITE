The Puk runs on a **CR2032** coin cell — the flat silver battery sold in every
supermarket and pharmacy. It lasts around a year and a half, and you replace it
yourself.

## The swap

1. Open the battery compartment.
2. Lift the old cell out.
3. Drop the new one in with the **+ side facing up**, the way the old one came
   out.
4. Close it.

The Puk wakes as soon as the cell touches its contacts and rejoins your Tali on
its own. Nothing needs re-pairing and no history is lost.

## Buying cells

Any brand of CR2032 is fine. Buy them somewhere with turnover — coin cells sold
loose from a dusty rack are often half-drained before you get them home, which
is a frustrating way to spend an afternoon chasing a sensor that was never
broken.

## Why it lasts so long

A Puk carries no Wi-Fi radio. It measures, whispers the result to your Tali over
Bluetooth, and goes back to sleep. That is the whole reason a battery this small
lasts this long.
